## First, Let's Acknowledge Something Important

You built a working web app that solves a real problem for Nigerian oil & gas engineers. That's genuinely hard, and most people never get that far. Now the question is: *how do you turn a working app into a sustainable business?* That's what this guide is about.

The short answer: **your numbers are really good — you just need enough users.**

---

## Part 1: How the Money Actually Works (Paystack and You)

Before we talk about profits and losses, you need to understand what you actually *keep* when someone pays you.

### Monthly Plan — ₦75,000/month
- A user pays you ₦75,000
- Paystack (your payment processor — think of them like a cashier that takes a small cut) charges ₦1,225 per transaction
- **You actually receive: ₦73,775**

### Annual Plan — ₦750,000/year
- A user pays you ₦750,000 upfront for the whole year
- Paystack charges ₦2,100 (they have a cap, so the bigger the amount, the less they take percentage-wise)
- **You actually receive: ₦747,900**

**What this means for you right now:** The annual plan is a great deal for both sides. Your user saves the equivalent of 2 months of fees. You get paid upfront (which helps your cash flow) and Paystack takes less. When you're talking to potential customers, lead with the annual plan.

## Part 2: Not Everyone Who Signs Up Will Pay — And That's Normal

Here's something that surprises a lot of first-time founders: out of every 100 people who create a free account on your app, you should expect about **5 of them to eventually pay**.

That's called your **conversion rate** — the percentage of free users who become paying customers.

5% sounds low, but it's actually *healthy* for a B2B SaaS product (that's just a fancy way of saying "software that businesses pay for on a subscription"). Many successful apps start at 2–3%.

**Think of it like a roadside shop.** A hundred people walk past. Five come in and buy something. The goal isn't to chase every passerby — it's to make sure the five who do come in spend well and come back.

**What this means for you right now:** Your job isn't to make every free user pay. Your job is to make the free experience good enough that the *right* people — the ones who really need it — see the value and upgrade.

**This is the toughest valley.** You're past "small side project" but not yet at "self-sustaining business." Almost every successful software company goes through this.

**Think of it like a corner shop expanding into a supermarket.** The moment you start stocking more shelves and hiring a cashier, your costs jump — but your revenue doesn't catch up immediately. That's normal. The question is: can you get through this valley?

You're still losing money, but look — you went from 100 to 1,000 paying users and the loss barely changed. That's because your costs don't grow as fast as your revenue does. The servers scale efficiently. You're approaching the tipping point.

## Part 6: Unit Economics — The "Health Checkup" for Your Business

These are the three numbers that tell you whether a subscription business is healthy or not. Investors obsess over these. Here's what they mean in plain English.

---

### LTV — "How Much Is One Customer Worth?"
**LTV (Lifetime Value): ₦1,770,600**

This is the total money you'd make from one customer over their entire relationship with you — assuming they stay for about 2 years.

If a customer pays ₦73,775/month and stays for 24 months, that's roughly ₦1.77 million. That's the "lifetime value" of one customer.

---

### CAC — "How Much Does It Cost to Get One Customer?"
**CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost): ₦50,000–₦150,000**

This is what you spend — on marketing, sales calls, demos, social media ads, your time — to bring in one paying customer.

Even at the high end (₦150,000), you recover that cost within 2 months of the customer paying you. After that, everything they pay is pure profit.

---

### LTV:CAC Ratio — "Are You Getting Good Value from Your Marketing?"
**Your ratio: 12:1 to 35:1**

For every ₦1 you spend to get a customer, you get back ₦12 to ₦35.

For context: investors get excited when this ratio is above **3:1**. You're at 12–35:1. That's exceptional.

**Think of it like planting seeds.** If you plant one seed and it grows 12 mangoes, you plant as many seeds as you can. That's what this ratio means for your marketing budget.

---

### Payback Period — "How Fast Do You Get Your Money Back?"
**1–2 months**

This is how quickly you recover what you spent to acquire a customer. Most software companies aim for under 12 months. You're recovering it in 1–2 months. That's very healthy.

---

### Gross Margin — "What Percentage of Each Sale Is Profit?"
**91% at 10,000 users**

For every ₦100 you earn, ₦91 is profit *before you pay your team*. The servers, AI costs, and payment fees only eat up ₦9.

For comparison: a restaurant might have 30% gross margin. A supermarket might have 20%. Software at 91% is why tech companies can be so valuable.

**What this means for you right now:** Your business model is fundamentally sound. These numbers don't lie. The only variable is how fast you can grow.

## Part 7: Break-Even — The Line Between Losing and Making Money

Break-even is the point where your revenue equals your costs. Below it, you're losing money. Above it, you're making money.

Here's what break-even looks like at each stage:

| Total Users | Paying Users Needed to Break Even | % Who Need to Pay |
|-------------|-----------------------------------|-------------------|
| 100 users | 10 paying users | 10% |
| 1,000 users | 56 paying users | 5.6% |
| 10,000 users | 177 paying users | 1.8% |

Notice something interesting: as you get bigger, the *percentage* who need to pay actually gets *smaller*. At 10,000 users, you only need 1.8% of them to pay — even less than your expected 5% conversion rate.

**What this means for you right now:** If you can hit 10,000 total users with a normal conversion rate, profitability becomes almost automatic. The scale works in your favor.

## Part 8: The Three Most Important Things to Do This Week

Not next quarter. Not after you raise money. **This week or next.**

---

### 1. Move Your LAS Files Off the Database (8–12 hours of work)

Right now, you're probably storing LAS well log files directly in your database. As you grow, this will get expensive fast — databases charge a lot for storage.

The fix: move those files to **Cloudflare R2**, a cheap cloud storage service (like a warehouse for files). It costs a fraction of what a database charges for the same storage.

**Why it matters:** At 1,000 users, this could save you ₦200,000–₦500,000 per month. That's not nothing.

---

### 2. Point Your API Directly to Render (4–6 hours of work)

Currently, requests from your users probably go: User → Vercel → Render (your server). That extra hop through Vercel adds a tiny delay to every request.

The fix: route users directly to Render. Your app will feel faster, and you'll cut out a middleman.

**Why it matters:** Faster app = happier users = lower churn (fewer people cancelling). Even a 0.5-second improvement in response time can meaningfully improve user satisfaction.

---

### 3. Focus on Converting Free Users to Paid

This is the highest-leverage thing you can do. Going from **5% to 8% conversion** at 10,000 users adds roughly **₦11 million per month** in revenue. That's not from getting more users — that's just from doing a better job of convincing the users you already have.

How to improve conversion:
- Email free users after they upload their first file: "Liked that? Here's what the Pro version gives you..."
- Show a teaser of Pro features but lock them behind payment
- Offer a 7-day free trial of Pro to engaged free users
- Talk to your free users — ask why they haven't paid. The answer will surprise you.

## Part 9: The Risks to Watch Out For

Being honest: here are the things that could hurt you.

### ⚠️ Power Users Who Upload Way More Than Average
If your typical user uploads 30 files a month but some power users upload 100+, your AI processing costs spike dramatically — up to 3× what you budgeted. 

**Fix:** Add usage limits or tiered pricing. Power users should pay more.

### ⚠️ Hiring the Wrong Engineers
If you hire US-based engineers as you scale, your costs jump by roughly ₦16 million per month compared to hiring offshore or Nigerian engineers.

**Fix:** Build a strong local team. Nigeria has excellent engineers. Use diaspora talent or remote-first African talent pools before going US/UK.

### ⚠️ If Conversion Drops to 2%
If for some reason your free-to-paid conversion drops to 2% instead of the expected 5%, you lose roughly ₦22 million per month in potential revenue at scale.

**Fix:** Monitor your conversion rate monthly. If it drops, investigate immediately. Talk to users. Fix the onboarding.

## Part 10: Why ₦75,000/Month Is the Right Price

You might worry: "Is this too expensive for Nigerian customers?" Let's put it in perspective.

- A **petrophysicist** (the type of engineer who uses your app) earns more than ₦75,000 *in a single day*
- A typical **well log analysis project** is worth tens of millions of naira. ₦75,000/month is less than 0.1% of that value
- Your users can pay with **local Nigerian cards** — no foreign exchange headaches, no dollar conversion
- The **annual plan** (₦750,000) saves users the equivalent of 2 months and saves you Paystack fees

In other words: you're charging a lot less than you probably could. This is a feature, not a problem. It makes the sale easier.
